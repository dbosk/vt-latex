\section{Related work}

\Textcite{Knauff2014Latex} provide the only controlled comparison of LaTeX
against a word processor we found; it measures efficiency and errors, not
conceptions, and studies professionals rather than learners.
Variation theory has been applied to computing education
\autocite{ThuneEckerdal2009Variation,ThuneEckerdal2018Lab,
Svensson2020Affordances}; our companion papers
\autocite{VTProgMisconceptions,VTDebug,VTTerminal} extend this line to
misconception catalogues for programming, debugging, and the shell.

Lacking a LaTeX-specific literature, we draw on three adjacent bodies of
work (the systematic search is documented in \cref{app:protocol}).
The comprehension of \emph{compiler and programming error messages} is
well studied: novices find such messages cryptic and a barrier, repeat
errors, and often fail to act on the first one
\autocite{Becker2016CEM,Traver2010CompilerErrors,%
Munson2016CompilerErrors,Denny2021ErrorReadability,Prather2017Interaction};
we transfer these findings to TeX errors in \cref{sec:errors}.
For \emph{markup in education}, empirical research is scarce even for HTML
and CSS \autocite{Kharchenko2025WebDesign}, and authoring structured
documents is a case of \emph{end-user programming}, whose learning
barriers are catalogued by \textcite{Ko2004Barriers}; both inform
\cref{sec:markup}.
None of these studies concerns LaTeX, and to our knowledge no misconception
catalogue exists for document preparation; this paper aims to be the first.
