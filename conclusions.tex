\rqmisconceptions*
So far: the literature is nearly empty---no study of learners'
misconceptions of LaTeX exists, and none of their understanding of it has
been measured (\cref{app:protocol,sec:protocol-measurement}); the seed
round found only the efficiency comparison \autocite{Knauff2014Latex}.
The catalogue is therefore built from adjacent literatures and course
data: difficulties with the formality and nesting of a neighbouring markup
language \autocite{Park2011WebDev,Park2016Hierarchies}, with reading
compiler error messages and acting on the first
\autocite{Becker2016CEM,Munson2016CompilerErrors,Prather2017Interaction},
with errors that stay latent when nothing reports them
\autocite{Park2015SyntaxErrors}, and the semantic-markup checks of our own
lab grading.

\rqaspects*
Ten candidate aspects, listed per objective in
\cref{sec:compilation-aspects,sec:errors-aspects,sec:markup-aspects}
and summarised in \cref{tab:aspects}; the overarching one is the compile
model that displaces the WYSIWYG prior.
Their evidential status is uneven and is stated with each: four are read
off difficulties documented in the literature---for a neighbouring markup
language or for programming errors, never for LaTeX itself---one off our
own course's grading, and five are posited from the structure of the
object of learning.
Every aspect has an item in the pre/post-test of \cref{app:quiz} (one has
two; four have open twins as well, the first because its closed form is
asked only at the end, and the error-message aspect has an open item of
its own), which is what will turn \enquote{candidate} into
\enquote{critical} or not.

\rqpatterns*
Preliminary patterns accompany each aspect; the
identical-look-diverging-behaviour contrast in \cref{sec:markup} is the
paradigm case for semantic markup.

The natural design for validating the aspects is a learning study
\autocite[Ch.~7]{NCOL} per objective, with the pre- and post-test of
\cref{app:quiz} as its instrument: the closed items test the aspects
argued for here, and the open accounts---the first phenomenographic data
on LaTeX that we know of---are where aspects we did not think of will
show themselves.
